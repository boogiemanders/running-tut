# Runner growth, retention and repeat buying: what it means for Running Tut

_Researched 2026-11-07. Every figure links to the page it was read from. Survey and market-research pages were read through a fetch tool; paid full reports (OIA, Running USA, MakerSights) were not, so only their public summaries are used. Brand homepage URLs are the brands' standard domains and were not each opened. Where two sources disagree, both numbers are shown._

## The short version

1. **More people run, but the growth is casual runners.** US road running hit 52.3 million participants in 2025. Casual running grew about 7% a year for three years, while core runners (50+ runs a year) slipped about 1.1%. Trail running is the fastest-growing part, up 57% since 2019. ([OIA](https://outdoorindustry.org/2026-running-report/))
2. **Young runners and run clubs are the momentum.** Strava running clubs grew 3.5x in 2025, and Gen Z race entries rose around 30% or more in every distance. ([SGB / Strava](https://sgbonline.com/stravas-year-in-sport-trend-report-highlights-gen-zs-passion-for-run/), [Athletech / Strava](https://athletechnews.com/running-walking-strength-training-strava-year-in-sport-report/))
3. **New runners quit a lot, mostly from injury.** About 30% of novices stopped within six months of a beginner program, and 48% of those stopped because of injury. ([Fokkema et al. 2019](https://read.qxmd.com/read/29934211/reasons-and-predictors-of-discontinuation-of-running-after-a-running-program-for-novice-runners))
4. **Runners are loyal to shoe brands, not apparel brands.** 75% walk into a store with a shoe brand in mind, but only 39% do for apparel. ([Formula4 Media](https://www.formula4media.com/new-articles/running-consumer-survey-trend-insight))
5. **Activewear repeat buying is better than fashion.** About 33% of activewear customers buy again within 12 months, and the top quartile is 40% or more. Typical gap between orders is 60 to 180 days. ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/average-repeat-purchase-rate-by-vertical))
6. **For Tut:** if runners like the brand, expect roughly 1 in 3 to buy again within a year, about every 3 to 4 months, if there is something new to buy. Because "luxury that lasts" means pieces don't wear out, repeat sales have to come from the range (pant, then tank, sleeve, belt) and drops, not from replacing worn kit.

## 6. What this means for Running Tut: "if they like it, will they buy again?"

**Short answer:** yes, but only about a third will in the first year, and only if Tut gives them something new to buy.

**A rough model (my estimate, built from the benchmarks above):**

- Out of 100 first-time customers, expect **25 to 40 to buy again within 12 months** (activewear average 33%, top quartile 40%+). Fewer if most are brand-new runners, more if they're marathon trainees from run clubs.
- Those repeaters typically order **every 2 to 4 months**, so **2 to 3 orders a year**.
- That works out to about **1.5 orders per customer in year one**. At a ~$200 pant (the price point from the earlier [offer research](2026-11-hormozi-market-offer-launch.md)), that's roughly **$300 of revenue per customer in year one**.
- Over time, returning customers should become **about 60% of revenue**, which is normal for apparel.

**Things that push Tut's number up or down:**

1. **Durability works against replacement buying.** "Luxury that lasts" means nobody re-buys the pant because it wore out. Repeat buys must come from a range: pant first, then tank, long sleeve, belt, and seasonal colourways. The 5-year repair/replace promise builds trust, and it can also bring people back to the site.
2. **The price is a big share of a runner's budget.** About half of runners spend $100 to $300 a year on all running apparel. One $200 Tut piece can be their whole year. Target the ~30% who spend $300+ (committed, racing runners), and offer cheaper second items (tank, sleeve, belt) so a second order is easy to say yes to.
3. **Target runners who have already stuck with it.** A third of novices quit within six months, mostly from injury. Marathon and half trainees have a "why" and race about 11 times a year, so they stay customers longer.
4. **Apparel loyalty is low, so community is the moat.** Only 39% of runners shop with an apparel brand in mind. The brands that break through (Tracksmith, Rabbit, Bandit) do it with community runs and a story. Strava running clubs grew 3.5x in 2025, so Tut run-club meetups are the cheapest loyalty program available.
5. **The second order happens fast or not at all.** Half of second orders come within 30 days and three quarters within 90. Plan follow-up for the first 90 days (fit check, care guide, run invite, next drop), and send a win-back at about day 100.
6. **Repeat-purchase tracking from day one.** Track 90-day and 12-month repeat rate by cohort. Below 15% means something is wrong; 33%+ means it's working; 40% is excellent.

## Open questions

- No source says how many years a typical runner keeps running. Ask in Anders' marathon-trainee interviews: "How long have you been running? Have you stopped before, and why?"
- No benchmark exists for premium running apparel specifically. Ask the interviewees how many running pieces they buy a year and how many from one brand.
- The full Running USA 2025 survey (paid) has spending and loyalty detail that would sharpen the model.
