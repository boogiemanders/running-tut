# Co-founder roles: Anders + Tut

Draft for dinner, 2026-11. Nothing here is decided until both of us agree.

## The short version

Anders' proposal is a good split. It follows the classic "maker + seller" pairing that most two-person apparel brands start with: one person owns what the product *is*, the other owns how people *find and buy it*. The split is clean and plays to each person's strengths.

What it misses is the unglamorous middle: getting the thing made, paying for it, shipping it, and looking after the people who bought it. In apparel, that middle is where most small brands die (late samples, bad factory runs, cash tied up in stock). Each of those needs one named owner, so below each gap gets an owner.

## Proposed ownership

One owner per area. "Owner" means that person decides and is accountable; the other person is consulted, not blocked.

| Area | Owner | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Product design** | Tut | Silhouettes, fabrics, colorways, details (pockets, gel loops, reflectors), the line plan |
| **Brand philosophy, vision, story** | Tut | Why we exist, what we refuse to make, the founding story, tone of the brand |
| **Runner truth / wear-testing** | Tut | Real miles in every sample, test-runner group, "does this actually work on a long run" sign-off |
| **Marketing and content** | Anders | Concept videos, social, launch plan, email list, partnerships, ads later |
| **Tech and storefront** | Anders | Website, Shopify (or similar), analytics, tools, the repo |
| **Sourcing and manufacturing** | Tut leads, Anders backs up | Finding fabric mills and factories, tech packs, sample rounds, production QC. Tut leads because it sits closest to design; Anders handles quotes, timelines and comparisons |
| **Operations and fulfillment** | Anders | Inventory, shipping, returns, packaging logistics, pre-order flow |
| **Finance and legal** | Anders | Bank account, bookkeeping, budget, pricing math (unit cost vs. price), company setup, trademark |
| **Customer service** | Anders, at first | Inbox, order problems, returns. Moves to whoever has more time once orders exist |
| **Community** | Shared, Tut is the face | Run club, group runs, events, the people who wear-test. Tut shows up as the runner; Anders runs the logistics and turns it into content |
| **Stitching and sample-making** | Both | Learn together so we can prototype fast and talk to factories credibly |

**Why this shape:** Tut keeps everything that decides *what the product is and feels like*. Anders takes everything that turns it into a business. Sourcing is the one real overlap, so it gets a lead and a backup.

### Watch-outs on this split

- **Anders' side is much bigger.** Marketing, tech, ops, finance and customer service is a lot. That's fine early (most of it is light until there are orders), but check it at the first pre-order. If it's too much, customer service or community logistics should move.
- **Sourcing is where design meets money.** Tut wants the best fabric; Anders sees the unit cost. Agree now on a target price and margin (rule of thumb for apparel: unit cost about 20–25% of retail) so "too expensive" is a number, not an argument.
- **"Brand" sits on both sides.** Tut owns what the brand *says*; Anders owns where and how it's *shown*. If a post feels off-brand, Tut can veto it. If a design is unsellable, Anders can flag it, but Tut decides.

## Decision rights

| Decision | Who decides |
|---|---|
| Day-to-day calls in your own area | The owner, no check-in needed |
| A product going into production | Both (Tut proposes, Anders confirms the numbers) |
| Spending over an agreed limit (suggest $500) | Both |
| Brand name, logo, core story changes | Both |
| Bringing in a partner, investor, employee or freelancer with equity | Both |
| A real deadlock | Owner of that area breaks the tie; if it's truly shared, sleep on it a week, then ask a trusted outside person |

## Equity and vesting (things to agree, not answers)

- **Split.** Equal (50/50) is the norm when two friends start together and both commit similar time. Unequal makes sense only if time, money or IP in are very different. Talk about it openly now while it's worth nothing.
- **Vesting.** Standard is 4 years with a 1-year cliff: if one of us leaves in year one, they keep nothing; after that, ownership builds monthly. It protects the person who stays. It feels awkward between friends, which is exactly why it's worth doing.
- **Time commitment.** Write down expected hours per week each. Mismatched effort is the #1 co-founder fight.
- **Money in.** If one of us puts in cash for samples or stock, decide whether it's a loan, extra equity, or just shared spend.
- **IP.** Designs, the name and content belong to the company, not to whoever made them.
- **Leaving.** What happens if one of us wants out, or stops pulling their weight? Who can buy the other out, and at what price?

## Co-founder agreement checklist

Before the first real money is spent or the first order is taken:

- [ ] Roles and owners above, agreed and written down
- [ ] Equity split
- [ ] Vesting schedule and cliff
- [ ] Hours per week each, and what "full-time" would trigger
- [ ] Money each puts in, and on what terms
- [ ] IP assignment to the company
- [ ] Spending limit and decision rules
- [ ] Deadlock rule
- [ ] Exit and buyout terms
- [ ] Company set up (LLC is the usual start), with a shared bank account
- [ ] Trademark search on the name
- [ ] Agreement reviewed by a lawyer before signing (a short consult is usually enough at this stage)
