# A modern Chinese running brand: landscape, gaps, and how it fits Tut

*Research note, 6 Nov 2026. Anders's idea: a modern Chinese running clothing brand, with Tut or solo.*

## The short version

- **The big Chinese players are shoe companies first.** Li-Ning, Anta, Xtep and 361° have won serious credibility on racing shoes (carbon plates, supercritical foams, national records). Their *apparel* is mostly mass-market and either generic or loud "guochao" (national-trend) graphics. Nobody owns "premium Chinese running apparel" in the West.
- **A new wave of Chinese indie brands is design-led but outdoor/trail first.** Outopia, Uppervoid, Kailas, Blue Monte and Aonijie are good, but they read as Arc'teryx/Satisfy-style technical outdoor, not as culturally Chinese.
- **Diaspora brands do culture, not running.** Chop Suey Club, SUM London, CLOT and others are proving that "modern Chinese" sells to Western buyers, but none of them makes serious running kit.
- **The gap:** a road/city running brand that is (1) genuinely performance-grade, (2) built to last, (3) Chinese in its design language rather than its logos, and (4) sold in English to diaspora and design-minded runners. That slots straight into the existing "luxury that lasts, with big pockets and gel loops" idea.

## 4. What's working

1. **Product first, culture second.** Xtep and Li-Ning only got respect once their racing shoes won races. A culture-led running brand with weak fabric would be a costume.
2. **Subtle beats loud.** The "China Li-Ning" giant-character look was a moment in 2018; the newer indies (Outopia, NEDAO) keep the cues in names, details and colour. Western runners buy quiet design.
3. **Community as distribution.** Xtep's running clubs, Bandit's club, Chinatown run clubs. A brand that starts with a club gets content and testers for free.
4. **The running boom in China is enormous.** Hundreds of marathons a year and a fast-growing trail scene, so Chinese factories now make world-class technical running fabric. That's a sourcing advantage.

## 5. Gaps a new brand could own

- **Premium, durable *road and city* running apparel that is recognisably Chinese in design.** Nobody is here.
- **Diaspora runners** (Chinese-American, Asian-American, and anyone into Chinese design) who currently buy Satisfy or Bandit and have nothing that speaks to them.
- **Fit for Asian bodies** in men's running kit (Maia Active proved it in women's).
- **Honest "Made in China, and proud of it."** Chop Suey Club and SUM London are already turning that phrase around; no running brand has.

## 6. Design language ideas (Chinese without being a costume)

- **Garment structure:** the *duijin* (对襟) centre-opening jacket, mandarin collar on a wind shell, frog-closure-inspired (盘扣) toggles on a vest, side slits from the *qipao* reworked as shorts vents.
- **Pockets with a story:** the wide inner sleeve pocket of a Hanfu robe (袖袋) as the inspiration for a big, bounce-free phone pocket. This ties the culture directly to our functional hook.
- **Colour:** traditional Chinese colour names (e.g. 黛蓝 dai-lan ink blue, 朱砂 cinnabar red, 竹青 bamboo green, 月白 moon white) as the colourway system. This is also the antidote to Hoka-style colourways.
- **Craft details:** *sashiko*-like reinforced stitching has a Chinese cousin in 纳鞋底 shoe-sole quilting; repair and mending as a lifetime-warranty story.
- **Typography:** small seal-stamp (印章) logo, one character, not a chest slogan.
- **Running culture:** dawn park runners, tai chi groups, Shanghai's Bund and Hong Kong's ridge trails as content locations.

## 7. Authenticity and cultural-appropriation check

How credible this is depends a lot on the founders' own connection to Chinese culture, so say that story plainly and personally. Either way it needs care:

- **Ground every motif in something real.** Name the source of each design cue (the garment, the dynasty, the place) on the product page. Avoid mixing Japanese and Chinese motifs, and avoid generic "Oriental" dragons, fortune cookies, chopstick fonts.
- **Work with Chinese makers and designers** and say so. Credit them by name.
- **Language:** get any Chinese text checked by native speakers (simplified vs traditional matters, and choosing one says something about who you're talking to: mainland vs Hong Kong/Taiwan/diaspora).
- **Politics:** Chinese brands are often pulled into geopolitics (e.g. the 2021 Xinjiang cotton boycotts, where Li-Ning and Anta gained in China while Western brands lost). Keep a clear, documented sourcing policy, avoid Xinjiang cotton, and stay out of nationalism. "Diaspora and craft" is safer than "national pride."
- **Don't blur cultures.** If the brand ever widens to "modern East Asian," that blend is exactly where appropriation complaints come from, so make it a deliberate choice, not a default.

## 8. How it fits the existing Running Tut positioning

The existing thesis is "luxury that lasts, with big pockets and gel loops." A Chinese angle strengthens each part:

| Big pockets | Inspired by Hanfu sleeve pockets: a story, not just a feature |

**First test (fits the launch plan):** one concept video for a mandarin-collar wind shell plus long tights with a "sleeve pocket," shot with a Chinatown run club. Compare views and saves against a non-Chinese concept video from the same account. That answers "does the Chinese angle add traction?" cheaply, before Tut has to commit either way.

## 9. Product concept: tops, an AirPods pocket and calligraphy reflectors

*Anders's ideas, 6 Nov 2026.*

### Sleeves and tanks
- **Tank / singlet:** mesh-backed racing tank with a dropped armhole. Calligraphy reflector centred on the upper back, on the spine line (where car headlights hit a runner from behind). Optional small one at centre chest.
- **Long sleeve:** thumbholes, mandarin collar option, calligraphy centred on the back (or centre chest). Plain, non-calligraphy reflective piping can go on the forearms and ankles, since moving limbs make a runner visible at night.
- **Arm sleeves (pull-on):** the cheapest product to prototype and the easiest to sell with a tank. Keep calligraphy off them (it would sit off-centre); use plain reflective piping or a small seal stamp instead, and let the matching tank carry the centred character.

### AirPods pocket
- Size it for the AirPods Pro case (about 61 × 45 × 22 mm), which also fits the smaller standard AirPods case. Target an inside pocket of roughly 70 × 55 mm with some stretch.
- **Where:** a zipped or flap pocket on the back of the waistband (pant and shorts), or a small chest/back pocket on the tank. Avoid the arm sleeve: a hard case flaps there.
- **Must do:** a closure (zip or magnetic flap), because an AirPods case is slippery and expensive to lose; a soft lining so it doesn't rattle; and a cord loop or key clip nearby for runners who clip the case on.
- This becomes part of the pocket story: "a place for everything you run with: phone, gels, keys, AirPods."

### Small calligraphy reflectors
**How to make them (options, simplest first):**
| Method | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reflective heat-transfer film**, cut and heat-pressed (e.g. 3M Scotchlite heat-transfer films) | Bold, simple strokes; cheapest to sample | Very thin, dry-brush stroke ends won't cut or weed cleanly. Keep strokes at least ~1 mm wide and test. |
| **Printable reflective film** (digitally printed, then cut) | Fine detail, ink-wash greys, coloured reflectors | Costlier per piece; check wash durability |
| **Reflective ink, screen-printed** | Fine strokes at volume; feels soft, stretches with the fabric | Needs a screen per design; less bright than film |
| **Laser-cut reflective tape or bonded patch** | Clean edges, seal-stamp shapes | Stiffer on stretch fabric |

All need wash-testing (reflective film can crack or peel after many washes, especially on high-stretch fabric). Ask 3M or a heat-transfer supplier for samples and data sheets before committing ([3M reflective heat transfer film](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_CA/p/d/b00020898/)).

**Design rules:**
- **Use the fonts Anders already picked** in his calligraphy lab (`thepsychology-ai`, `src/app/lab/calligraphy/components/fonts.ts`). See "Fonts from the calligraphy lab" below for which suit reflectors.
- **Always centred (Anders's rule):** calligraphy goes on the centre line only, centre back or centre chest, never off to one side. Off-centre characters look wrong. The one exception is a smaller character placed on a pocket (e.g. the thigh phone pocket or the tank's AirPods pocket), centred on that pocket. Anything on a side, sleeve or leg uses plain reflective trim instead.
- **Small and quiet:** one or two characters, a few centimetres tall. Reflects bright at night, nearly invisible by day.
- **Grey or black reflective**, not silver, if you want it subtle in daylight.

**Picking the characters (what to watch for):**
- Have several native speakers, from both the mainland and the diaspora, check every character before printing. Mistakes go viral (the classic "tattoo that doesn't mean what you think").
- **Hidden meanings:** 疾 means "swift" but its main meaning today is "illness." 四 (four) sounds like 死 (death). Avoid characters with a negative second meaning, and don't use the number 4.
- **Stroke direction and mirroring:** check the artwork isn't flipped during heat-transfer (a common printing error).
- **Simplified vs traditional:** decide once and stay consistent. It signals who you're talking to.
- **Stay out of politics and religion:** avoid slogans, nationalist phrases, and religious terms used as decoration.
- **Good candidate characters to test** (each needs native-speaker sign-off): 跑 run, 风 wind, 光 light, 夜 night, 远 far, 行 go/travel, 恒 perseverance. A "night series" with 夜 and 光 on reflectors is a neat story.

### Fonts from the calligraphy lab

Anders's lab has 23 fonts in two groups. For reflectors, stroke weight matters: cut reflective film needs strokes of roughly 1 mm or more, so the **brush** group suits a few-centimetre character better than the thin **pen** group.

**Brush 毛笔 (best fit for reflectors):** Sun GuoTing ShuPu (classical characters only), ZhiMang Xing, SuXin ShiShuBiao, XiaoDouDao MoMoZhi, Li XuKe ShuFa, HanCheng WangShiLi XingShu, ZiXiaoHun FengYi ShouShu, CaoTanZhai Mao ZeDong, DuanNing MaoBi XingShu, MiNi Fan ZhiCao, ZhongQi LiQuan CaoShu, ShuTiFang Deng XiaoPing, FangZheng XingTi CaoShu (and its Jian simplified version).

**Pen 硬笔 (better for printed labels, hang tags, the website):** ZhangYu XiaoRouWan, YingZhang XingShu, TianWei LiangNiWeiXiao, XiaoDouDao QiuRiHeJianFan, Li GuoFu, HanCheng BoBo YingCao, YuWei ShuFa XingShu, ChenJiShi XingKai, ShangShou HongZhi.

**Two flags before using them on product:**
- **Skip the two fonts styled on political leaders' handwriting** (CaoTanZhai Mao ZeDong and ShuTiFang Deng XiaoPing). On clothing they carry political meaning to anyone who recognises them, which goes against the "stay out of politics" rule above.
- **Check each font's commercial license.** Printing on clothing you sell is commercial use. ZhiMang Xing is on Google Fonts under the open SIL license, so it's safe. FangZheng (方正) fonts are commercial and need a paid license. Many of the others are free only for personal use. Clear the license for whichever ones make the shortlist, or have the final characters redrawn by hand, using the font as reference.
- **Shortlist to try first:** ZhiMang Xing (open license, bold strokes), plus whichever two or three of the brush fonts look best at reflector size once their licenses check out.
